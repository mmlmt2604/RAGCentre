# Looking After Your Bike

A few minutes of care each week keeps your bike safe and makes its parts last longer.

## Before every ride

Squeeze both brake levers: they should feel firm and stop well before touching the handlebar.
Check that the quick-release levers or thru-axles are closed tightly. Spin each wheel and watch for
wobbles or rubbing brake pads.

## Tyre pressure

Check tyre pressure once a week, because tyres lose air slowly even without a puncture. Road
tyres usually run between 5 and 7 bar, gravel tyres between 2 and 3.5 bar, and mountain bike
tyres between 1.5 and 2 bar. The recommended range is printed on the tyre sidewall. Lower
pressure gives more grip and comfort; higher pressure rolls faster on smooth roads.

## Chain care

Wipe the chain with a dry rag after wet rides and apply chain lubricant every 150 to 300
kilometres, or whenever it starts to squeak. Apply one drop per roller, turn the pedals for a
minute, then wipe off the excess: oil on the outside of the chain only attracts dirt. Replace the
chain when a chain checker shows 0.5 percent wear, which protects the cassette and chainrings
from wearing out early.

## Cleaning

Use a bucket of warm water with a little bike cleaner and a soft brush. Never point a pressure
washer at the bearings in the hubs, headset or bottom bracket, because it forces water past the
seals. Dry the bike and lubricate the chain after washing.

## E-bike batteries

Store the battery indoors between 10 and 20 degrees Celsius. For storage longer than a month,
leave it charged to between 30 and 60 percent and top it up every eight weeks. Do not leave the
battery on the charger for days at a time.

## Servicing

We recommend a full service every 12 months or 3000 kilometres, whichever comes first. The first
service, six weeks after purchase, is free: cables stretch and bolts settle during the first few
rides.
